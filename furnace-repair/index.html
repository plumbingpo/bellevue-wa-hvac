From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Bellevue homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, measuring each stage as it happens. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. The safety checks come standard on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard rarely shows itself at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower work together the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Bellevue

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, so airflow issues are caught before they cut years off the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. The result is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
